# INT-005 — Sign in and sign out

> Derived from [[US-005]]. The work item is the source of truth; this is the
> originator's account of what they actually meant.

## What I want

Someone who already has an account comes back. They give their email and password
and the place recognises them — the right things appear, their cart is where they
left it, and they get on with what they came for.

And when they are done, or they are on someone else's machine, they can leave
properly. Leaving has to mean something: after it, nothing that needed an account
still works.

## Who it is for

People who registered already. Buyers and sellers both — the difference shows up
after they are in, not during.

## What "better" looks like

I come back tomorrow and I am still signed in. Being asked to sign in again every
morning, on the same machine, for a shop I have not bought anything from yet, is
the kind of small friction that makes people stop coming back.

## What worries me most

Getting the refusal wrong.

If a wrong email says something different from a wrong password, then anyone can
sit there with a list of addresses and find out which ones have accounts here. That
is a leak, even though nothing was stolen. **Both must fail identically** — same
words, same behaviour, and please, not something helpful like "no account with that
email".

I know this makes the message less useful to someone who genuinely mistyped their
address. That is the trade, and I am making it on purpose.

## What I do not want yet

Remembering the device, a second factor, a way back in for someone who forgot their
password, or any notion of locking an account after too many attempts. That last one
is a real gap and I would rather name it than pretend I have not noticed.

## What is out of my hands

How long someone stays signed in before they are asked again, and what happens if
their session runs out in the middle of something. I do not know enough to set that
number. Someone should pick one, write down why, and make sure that when it does run
out the person is told rather than silently finding that a button no longer works.
